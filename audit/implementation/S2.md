## 2. Common game, BR rule, and Nash set

Every new game has state `x=[x^1,x^2]^T`, zero additive payoff constants, unit
sensitivities, and symmetric quadratic representatives

```text
A^{1j}=[-1,p_j;p_j,-1],  b^{1j}=[c_j;0],
A^{2j}=[-1,q_j;q_j,-1],  b^{2j}=[0;d_j].
```

Thus each own curvature is `-1`, and

```text
h_j^1=-x^1+p_j x^2+c_j,    BR_j^1: x^1=p_j x^2+c_j,
h_j^2= q_j x^1-x^2+d_j,    BR_j^2: x^2=q_j x^1+d_j.
```

The implemented pseudo-gradient is the thesis nearest-BR rule (3.8): an agent is
inactive when its two own-gradients have opposite signs or one is zero; otherwise
it selects the smaller `|h_j^i|`, with objective 1 on a tie. Because every own
curvature has the same magnitude and every alpha is one, nearest-BR and scaled
own-gradient ordering agree for these constructed examples.

For every example the Nash set is stated globally, not estimated from pixels:

```text
X*(J)={x : h_1^1(x)h_2^1(x)<=0 and h_1^2(x)h_2^2(x)<=0}.
```

For an active pair `(j1,j2)`, the branch matrix and its scalar Nash point are

```text
A_(j1,j2)=[-1,p_j1;q_j2,-1],
det A_(j1,j2)=1-p_j1 q_j2,
x_*^(j1,j2)=-A_(j1,j2)^(-1)[c_j1;d_j2].
```

The complete 20-branch matrices, determinants, eigenvalues, and scalar corners are
in `audit/implementation/s2_branch_diagnostics.csv`.

## 3. Fig. 3.6 content: five cases under Assumption 4

All five games use `c=(0,0)` and `d=(1,-1)`. Therefore the two agent-1 BRs meet at
`x^p1=(0,0)`, where `(h_1^1,h_2^1)=(0,0)` while the agent-2 own-gradients are
`(1,-1)`. Hence `x^p1 in X*(J)` and agent 1 has rank zero there, directly
certifying Assumption 4. General position was checked pairwise: no BR pair is
parallel, no triple intersection exists, and no BR is horizontal or vertical.

| Case | `(p_1,p_2)` | `(q_1,q_2)` | determinant range | other same-agent intersection | mathematical identity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| D1 | `(.8,.3)` | `(-.6,-.2)` | `[1.06,1.48]` | `(5,-2)`, not Nash | stable; complex branches, clockwise one-target transitions |
| D2 | `(.6,.4)` | `(.8,.2)` | `[.52,.92]` | `(-3.3333,-1.6667)`, not Nash | stable; real eigenvectors, 0/2-target transition distinction |
| D3 | `(-.3,-.7)` | `(.8,.5)` | `[1.15,1.56]` | `(-6.6667,-4.3333)`, not Nash | stable; complex branches, counterclockwise transitions |
| D4 | `(.8,.5)` | `(-.4,.3)` | `[.76,1.32]` | `(2.8571,-.1429)`, not Nash | stable; mixed rotation and real-eigenvector transition types |
| D5 | `(1.1,1.3)` | `(2,1.6)` | `[-1.60,-.76]` | `(-5,-9)`, not Nash | unstable; four saddle branches |

The `p,q` ordering and branch matrices are the already certified S1 five-case
identities; S2 changes the intercept geometry so that the agent-1 BR intersection
is a rank-zero Nash pinch. This is the intended comparison with S1: the five
stability identities remain different even though the compact Nash geometry now
has disconnected interior at the pinch.

Theorem 3.11 applies because Assumptions 1, 2, and 4 were checked. D1–D4 have four
positive determinants and are asymptotically stable; D5 has four negative
determinants and is unstable. This classification does not depend on a trajectory
endpoint. Representative fixed-step RK4 trajectories are display evidence only:

| Case | one recorded `x(0)` | `x(7.005)` | interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| D1 | `(-2,-1)` | `(-.6452,-.8049)` | inward/asymptotic approach to the pinched Nash set |
| D2 | `(-2,2)` | `(.3390,.8523)` | real-eigenvector stable geometry |
| D3 | `(2,-1)` | `(.3893,-.5539)` | counterclockwise stable geometry |
| D4 | `(2,2)` | `(.5572,.6936)` | mixed-transition stable geometry |
| D5 | `(-2,-1)` | `(-16.7619,-20.6453)` | outward representative; max norm `26.5930` |

The finite final times in D1–D4 are not asserted to lie exactly in `X*(J)`;
Theorem 3.11 supplies the asymptotic statement. All plotted starts and endpoints
are recorded in `audit/implementation/s2_trajectory_summary.csv`.

## 4. Fig. 3.7 content: three noncompact configurations

These examples are deliberately new. They preserve the main visual distinctions
of a narrow one-saddle funnel, a broader three-saddle funnel, and opposite wings,
without claiming original parameters.

| Game | `p`; `q` | `c`; `d` | determinants `(11,12,21,22)` | Nash/recession geometry | representative trajectories |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| N1 | `(.4,1.6)`; `(.5,1)` | `(-1,1)`; `(-1,1)` | `(.8,.6,.2,-.6)` | narrow opposite unbounded funnels; sampled recession angle `25.99°` | `(-8,10)->(-1.0217,-.0504)`; `(0,0)` is stationary Nash |
| N2 | `(.6,1.4)`; `(.8,2)` | `(-2,1)`; `(1,-2)` | `(.52,-.2,-.12,-1.8)` | broad crossed funnel with three saddle branches; sampled recession angle `40.69°` | `(-8,13)->(-1.1060,.1236)`; `(8,13)` is stationary Nash |
| N3 | `(-.8,.8)`; `(-.7,1.8)` | `(-1,1)`; `(1,-1)` | `(.44,2.44,1.56,-.44)` | opposite-wing geometry; sampled recession angle `19.19°` | `(8,-10)->(.8258,.3627)`; `(8,10)` is stationary Nash |

For all three, Assumption 1 is true and the determinant signs are mixed. Therefore
Proposition 3.1 certifies that `X*(J)` is noncompact. The recession-angle samples
only describe the constructed geometry; they are not the compactness proof.

Assumption 2 fails, and neither antecedent of Theorem 3.11 is satisfied: the four
determinants are neither all positive nor all negative. Consequently Theorem 3.11
does not classify these complete piecewise systems as stable or unstable. Individual
saddle branches and finite trajectories cannot be promoted to a global theorem.
The observed convergence to different points of an unbounded Nash set is consistent
with the thesis discussion, but it is reported only as numerical behavior.
