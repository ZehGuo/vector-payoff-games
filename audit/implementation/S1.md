## 1. Controlling definitions and source reading

The construction uses the M0 contract: `J_j^i` has agent `i` in the superscript,
objective `j` in the subscript, and state order `x=[x^1,x^2]^T`. The selected
pseudo-gradient is the nearest-BR rule in thesis (3.8), including objective 1 on a
tie and zero motion when the two own-gradients have opposite signs or one is zero.

The following thesis material was read in the PDF and visually checked rather than
inferred from extracted text order:

- Lemma 3.6 and Fig. 3.3, printed pp. 36–37 (PDF pp. 42–43): the location of a real
  eigenvector relative to the two BR directions.
- Remarks 3.7–3.9, printed pp. 38–39 (PDF pp. 44–45): 0/1/2-transitive domains and
  the five BR-slope orderings.
- Theorem 3.10, printed pp. 39–40 (PDF pp. 45–46): under Assumptions 1–3, four
  all-positive-determinant cases are asymptotically stable and the
  all-negative-determinant case is unstable.
- Fig. 3.5, printed p. 44 (PDF p. 50): the intended five-panel comparison. The new
  parameters and layout below are not claimed to reproduce that figure exactly.

For a selected objective pair `(j1,j2)`, the local double-active branch is

`dot y = A_(j1,j2)y`, with `A_(j1,j2)=[-1,s_j1^1;s_j2^2,-1]`.

Thus `det A=1-s_j1^1 s_j2^2` and
`lambda_±=-1±sqrt(s_j1^1 s_j2^2)`. If the product is negative, the pair is
`-1±i sqrt(-s_j1^1 s_j2^2)` and there are no real eigenvectors to report.

## 2. New common construction and complete parameters

All five games use unit sensitivities. Own curvatures are `-1`, so nearest-BR and
scaled-own-gradient selection have the same global ordering (M0's equality
condition is satisfied). The two BRs of agent 1 meet at `p1=(5,6)`; those of agent 2
meet at `p2=(6,5)`. For either agent and objective with slope `s`, use

- agent 1: `A^{1j}=[-1,s;s,-1]`, `b^{1j}=[5-6s;0]`, so
  `BR_j^1: x^1=s x^2+(5-6s)`;
- agent 2: `A^{2j}=[-1,s;s,-1]`, `b^{2j}=[0;5-6s]`, so
  `BR_j^2: x^2=s x^1+(5-6s)`;
- all additive payoff constants are zero and all `alpha_j^i=1`.

This gives full symmetric quadratic payoff representatives, not just line sketches.
Only the owning row enters the pseudo-gradient, as required by M0.

All four lines in every example are in general position: no line is horizontal or
vertical, no pair is parallel, and no triple intersection occurs. Neither `p1` nor
`p2` belongs to the Nash set, so the same-agent own-gradient vector never has rank
zero on the Nash set (Assumption 3 geometry).

## 4. Nash geometry and domain transitions

For every case,

`X*(J)={x: h_1^1(x)h_2^1(x)<=0 and h_1^2(x)h_2^2(x)<=0}`.

It is the compact purple quadrilateral whose vertices are the four scalar-payoff Nash
points `x_*^{j1j2}=BR_j1^1 ∩ BR_j2^2`:

is the condition used by Theorem 3.10 for asymptotic stability of the set, while the
all-negative pattern is its unstable case. No conclusion here is extended to mixed
determinant signs, which Remark 3.9/Theorem 3.10 do not cover.

## 6. Coordinate-transformation boundary

A coordinate transformation can simplify domains and support a stability analysis;
it does not alter Case 5's positive eigenvalue in the original active branch and does
not automatically make the original system stable. In particular, a non-bijective map
may collapse information, so apparent convergence of a transformed image cannot erase
the red positive-eigenray witness. This S1 statement is a scope guard for later X1,
not an implementation of X1.
