## 1. Definitions and source contract

The payoff order is always

`[J_1^1, J_2^1, J_1^2, J_2^2] = [J11,J12,J21,J22]`,

with state `x=[x^1,x^2]^T`. For each payoff,

`dJ_j^i/dt = own_j^i + externality_j^i`

where

- `own_j^i=(partial J_j^i/partial x^i) f^i >= 0` under the selected pseudo-gradient;
- `externality_j^i=(partial J_j^i/partial x^{-i}) f^{-i}`;
- `Gamma_j^i={x: externality_j^i>=0}`;
- `Omega_j^i={x: dJ_j^i/dt>=0}`.

Thus `Gamma_j^i` is a sufficient externality certificate and `Gamma_j^i subset Omega_j^i`, but it is not the same set as `Omega_j^i`. For weak improvement of one agent the relevant sets are the unions `Gamma_1^i union Gamma_2^i` and `Omega_1^i union Omega_2^i`; for all-payoff nondecrease they are the four-way intersections. This is the distinction made in thesis (4.3)–(4.6), Definition 5, Fig. 4.1, and the trajectory condition following Fig. 4.2. ACC Definitions 2–3 separately distinguish instantaneous weak improvement from a two-time trap.

The thesis Fig. 4.3 page and numerical text were visually checked. Because M0 established that its literal agent/objective ordering and the surviving `cdc2024` arrays do not identify one unique figure-generating version, none of the first three panels is called an exact Fig. 4.3 reproduction. This avoids reviving the withdrawn Q1 paper transcription.

## 2. Three controlled payoff-property games

All three games have the same own gradients, nearest-BR rule, initial state, and exact trajectory. Only the four linear externality coefficients change. This isolates the difference between own improvement and total payoff change.

For payoff order `11,12,21,22`, use

| payoff | matrix `A` | linear term `b` before external coefficient is substituted |
|---|---|---|
| `J_1^1` | `[-1,0;0,0]` | `[2,e11]` |
| `J_2^1` | `[-1,0;0,0]` | `[4,e12]` |
| `J_1^2` | `[0,0;0,-1]` | `[e21,-1]` |
| `J_2^2` | `[0,0;0,-1]` | `[e22,1]` |

All constants are zero, all sensitivities are one, `x0=(0,3)`, and `t in [0,5]`. The matrices are strictly concave in each owner's own coordinate but only semidefinite in the full state. Therefore the evidence below uses direct identities (4.4)–(4.6), not Assumption 5 or Theorem 4.3.

| group | `(e11,e12,e21,e22)` | exact identity |
|---|---:|---|
| nonweak | `(5,6,-7,-6)` | both total derivatives are negative for both agents |
| all objectives nondecreasing | `(0,0,0,0)` | every externality is zero and every total derivative equals its nonnegative own contribution |
| weak but not all | `(3,-4,5,-3)` | objectives `J_1^1,J_2^2` decrease while `J_2^1,J_1^2` increase |

### 2.1 Exact trajectory and continuous-time sign certificates

The nearest BR is objective 1 for agent 1 and objective 2 for agent 2 throughout the nonstationary trajectory. With `z=exp(-t)`,

`x^1(t)=2(1-z), x^2(t)=1+2z, f(t)=(2z,-2z)`.

The four own-direction contributions are

`[4z^2, 4z+4z^2, 4z+4z^2, 4z^2]`,

and hence are strictly positive for every finite `t`. The total rates are:

| group | `[dJ11,dJ12,dJ21,dJ22]/dt` for `z in (0,1]` | conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| nonweak | `[4z^2-10z, 4z^2-8z, 4z^2-10z, 4z^2-12z]` | all four are negative; either agent is a same-agent two-negative-derivative witness |
| all | `[4z^2, 4z+4z^2, 4z+4z^2, 4z^2]` | all four are positive for finite `t` and tend to zero |
| weak not all | `[4z^2-6z, 4z^2+12z, 4z^2+14z, 4z^2-6z]` | exactly one objective per agent is negative and the other is positive |

These are continuous-time algebraic sign arguments, not conclusions inferred from a finite plot. The plotted samples only illustrate them.

### 2.2 Gamma versus Omega in the construction

- Nonweak: along the trajectory the externalities are `[-10z,-12z,-14z,-12z]`. Both `Gamma` unions fail, positive own contributions are too small, and both `Omega` unions also fail. This is the required nonweak group, independent of the trap.
- All: every externality is zero, so the four-way `Gamma` intersection is the whole state space. The nonnegative own terms then make the four-way `Omega` intersection the whole state space.
- Weak not all: externalities are `[-6z,8z,10z,-6z]`. Each agent always has one nonnegative externality, so both `Gamma` unions are the whole state space; hence both `Omega` unions are also the whole state space. The negative total rates of `J_1^1` and `J_2^2` prove that the four-way `Omega` intersection is not the whole state space.

This explicitly separates the externality certificate, total derivative, and own-direction contribution instead of coloring a point outside `Gamma` as automatically outside `Omega`.

## 3. Independent weak Pareto trap

The trap keeps the actual arrays generated by `trap.m` and `testfun1.m` and the exact helper behavior in `testfun2.m`:

| payoff | `A` | `b` | additive `c` |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `J_1^1` | `[-2,1;1,-3]` | `[5,-5]` | 0 |
| `J_2^1` | `[-2,-1;-1,-10]` | `[20,124]` | 0 |
| `J_1^2` | `[-4,1;1,-4]` | `[90,0]` | 0 in executable |
| `J_2^2` | `[-5,-1;-1,-2]` | `[72,0]` | 0 in executable |

`x0=(18.5,-8.71)`, `t in [0,4]`, and `alpha=[1,1.2,1,2]` in payoff order `11,12,21,22`. Each agent moves only when its two scaled own-gradients have the same strict sign, and chooses the smaller absolute scaled gradient, with objective 1 on a tie. This is the **scaled-own-gradient** rule. It is not relabeled as the nearest-BR equation in the thesis/ACC: for agent 1 the curvature-scalings are `2` and `2.4`, so M0's global equivalence condition fails.

The thesis caption records different sensitivities and `t1=0,t2=2.75`. Per the author's review, those caption differences do not authorize replacing the actual simulation version. The experiment instead searches its own sampled trajectory for an explicit non-endpoint witness:

| quantity | `t1=0.020` | `t2=1.819` | `value(t2)-value(t1)` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `J_1^2` | 666.145167 | 665.829551 | -0.315616 |
| `J_2^2` | 563.841888 | 563.526847 | -0.315041 |

Thus `t1<t2` and both objectives of agent 2 are worse at `t2`. Neither time is the final time, and `t1` is strictly inside the integration interval. The thesis caption prints additive constants, while `trap.m` evaluates without them; the executable convention is used here. Adding constants would not affect the differences or the vector field.

On the independent `dt=0.001` RK4 output grid, the minimum of `max(dJ_1^i/dt,dJ_2^i/dt)` is `2.652905` for agent 1 and `0.029604` for agent 2. Hence every sampled instant has at least one nondecreasing objective per agent, while the two-time table gives the trap. This last statement is intentionally labeled **finite-grid numerical evidence**, not a continuous-time proof. The phase panel also distinguishes the agent-2 `Gamma`-failure region (both externalities negative) from the smaller `Omega`-failure region (both total rates negative); the sampled trajectory avoids the latter.
