# X1 public-figure captions and provenance

These figures are generated by MATLAB R2025b Update 7 through
`experiments/run_x1_transformation_stability.m`. The technical figures retain
the complete audit view; the `900` and `390` derivatives are reading aids for
repository, homepage, and narrow mobile layouts. X1 fiber collapse is a public
candidate after RR-04, but the final homepage selection remains an author
decision.

## Required public sentence

The map compresses two-dimensional domains to local quadrant subsets,
one-active strips to axes, and the Nash set to the origin. An axis point
therefore has an entire set-valued fiber as its inverse, so pointwise
`eta(x(t))` and independently integrated `z(t)` are different mathematical
objects.

## Technical figures

### `X1_domain_map.png`

**Caption.** Here `D^(i,j)` records the status of agents 1 and 2: `0` means the
agent is inactive (already in its Pareto-optimal strip), while `1` or `2` names
the selected objective. The displayed finite original-space window maps to
local subsets of the four quadrants, four half-axes, and the origin. Outlines,
markers, and direct labels supplement color.

- Evidence: the map in equations (43), (48), and (49), plus the sampled image of
  the displayed local window.
- Do not infer: the colored two-dimensional images do not assert surjectivity
  onto whole quadrants.
- Alt text: A labeled original partition maps to outlined and differently marked
  local quadrant subsets, styled half-axes, and one purple origin.

### `X1_fiber_collapse.png`

**Caption.** For a one-active domain, define the level fiber
`L_kappa^(i,j) = {x in D^(i,j) : eta^a(x)=kappa}`, where `a` is the active
coordinate. Every marked state on that fiber has one common axis image. Thus
the inverse at that axis point is the entire set-valued fiber; no representative
original state is selected. The Nash set similarly maps to `(0,0)`.

- Evidence: the rank-one restrictions in equation (49) and direct evaluation of
  the implemented map.
- Do not infer: there is no single-valued inverse on an axis.
- Alt text: Four colored original-space line fibers contain many open-circle
  states; each color becomes one filled point on a transformed coordinate axis.

### `X1_trajectories_lyapunov.png`

**Caption.** Read the panels as four distinct objects: (1) independently
integrated original `x(t)`; (2) pointwise `eta(x(t))`, shown solid with circles;
(3) independently integrated `z(t)`, shown dashed with triangles; and (4) the
piecewise-quadratic certificate evaluated along the two eta-space curves. The
A/B inset makes their deliberate post-fiber separation visible. Temporary
growth of the Euclidean norm is not, by itself, instability.

- Evidence: two separate fixed-step RK4 integrations, pointwise map evaluation,
  and verification of the printed four-branch certificate.
- Do not infer: the transformation is not a controller or stabilizer; a
  trajectory tending toward the origin does not replace a global stability
  theorem.
- Source discrepancies retained: the paper prints `eta_0^2=-13.5`, while direct
  evaluation gives `-13.25`; the printed A/B norm inequality has the opposite
  direction from the displayed labels and both recomputations.
- Alt text: Four numbered panels separate the original trajectory, its
  pointwise image, an independently integrated transformed trajectory, and
  certificate values; solid circles and dashed triangles visibly differ.

## Mobile/homepage derivatives

Every basename below is delivered in `_900.png` and `_390.png` variants.

### `X1_fiber_collapse_vertical`

**Caption.** Many original states on one level fiber share the same axis image.
The inverse of that image point is the full fiber, not one chosen state.

- One-message card: non-bijective many-to-one information loss.
- Alt text: A vertical two-panel card shows six original states on one colored
  fiber above and their one common filled axis point below.

### `X1_original_x_card`

**Caption.** Object 1 is `x(t)`, integrated in the original state space. The
orange A-to-B interval is one-active.

### `X1_pointwise_eta_card`

**Caption.** Object 2 is the solid, circle-marked pointwise image `eta(x(t))`.
It is evaluated after integrating `x(t)` and is not a second ODE solution.

### `X1_independent_z_card`

**Caption.** Object 3 is the dashed, triangle-marked `z(t)`, independently
integrated from `z(0)=eta(x_0)`. No representative of an axis fiber is chosen.

### `X1_certificate_card`

**Caption.** Object 4 is the piecewise-quadratic certificate evaluated along
the two eta-space curves. Euclidean-norm growth can occur temporarily and is
not, by itself, an instability conclusion.

## Nearby boundary statement

Use these statements together whenever the trajectory group is shown publicly:

`original system behavior | certificate applicability | non-bijective information loss`

They are three different questions. The coordinate transformation is an
analysis device, not a controller or stabilizer.
